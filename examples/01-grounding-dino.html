Grounding DINO: Marrying DINO with Grounded Pre-Training for Open-Set Object Detection

A DINO detector that reads a text prompt and fuses it with the image at three places, so that any phrase in the prompt can become a detection class.

The sixty second read

Everything below this block is optional.

The problem it names

Detect any object named in free text, a category list or a referring expression, with one model. Earlier open-set detectors fused language late, only at the classifier head, which limits how much the text can shape the visual features.

The mechanism

Fuse language into DINO three times: a feature enhancer with image-to-text and text-to-image cross-attention, language-guided query selection that picks the 900 image tokens most similar to any word, and an extra text cross-attention in every decoder layer. Classes are scored by a dot product between query and word features.

Headline result

52.5

COCO AP zero-shot with Swin-L, pre-trained on O365, OpenImages and GoldG, against 49.8 for GLIP-L (Table 2). Zero-shot here means no COCO images, not unseen categories.

What is actually new

Mostly the placement of fusion inside a strong DETR-style detector, plus a sub-sentence attention mask so category names in one prompt do not attend to each other. The individual pieces (bi-attention fusion, contrastive word heads, grounded pre-training) come from GLIP and MDETR.

The honest verdict

A very strong, widely used open-set baseline, built by careful integration rather than one new idea. The ablation gains are small, and the training recipe cannot be reproduced from the release.

Why it matters to you

It is the default baseline a reviewer will expect for unseen aerial classes, and it does nothing for small objects: no tiling, no resolution change, 900 queries per image. Its weak rare-class LVIS numbers (APr 18.1 for T) are the closest proxy for your novel aerial classes.

How it decides, in one picture

Two masks do most of the work. On the left, the prompt "small car . boat ." is split at every period: words attend only inside their own phrase. On the right, every image token is scored by its best match against any word, and only the top 900 become decoder queries. A small object that never wins a top-900 slot is never detected, whatever the decoder does later.

Where it stops, and where your work starts

Grounding DINO decides what to look at before it decides what things are: the query selection step keeps 900 image tokens per image, chosen by text similarity. That is fine for COCO, where objects are big and few. For aerial frames with hundreds of tiny vehicles, recall is capped by that budget and by the encoder's feature resolution, and nothing in the paper measures it. Its "zero-shot" claims also lean on Objects365 and GoldG, which already cover most COCO categories, so the paper says little about classes that are truly unseen. Small objects and truly novel classes are both outside what it tests.

The one sentence to put in your related work

Grounding DINO fuses language into the encoder, query selection and decoder of a DETR-style detector and reaches 52.5 zero-shot AP on COCO, but it keeps a fixed budget of 900 queries per image and is evaluated on benchmarks dominated by medium and large objects whose categories overlap its pre-training data.

The full breakdown

01Problem setup, and what "open-set" means hereOpen-set in this paper means "named by text", not "never seen in training".+

Input is an image and a text prompt. Output is a set of boxes, each paired with the noun phrase in the prompt it matches. For ordinary detection the prompt is the category names joined by periods ("car . boat . tank ."); for referring expressions it is a sentence, and the top-scoring box is returned.

There is no fixed label space. Classification is replaced by a similarity score between each decoder query and each word token, so a new class only needs a new word. This is the GLIP formulation, applied to a DINO detector instead of a DyHead one.

Read "zero-shot" carefully

Every zero-shot COCO number is transfer from a model trained on Objects365 and grounding data, which share most of COCO's 80 categories. It is zero-shot with respect to COCO images, not with respect to categories. The LVIS rare split and ODinW are the closer tests of unseen concepts, and both are much weaker (Tables 3 and 4).

02Feature enhancer, fusion in the encoderRemoving it is the most expensive ablation, and it hurts LVIS three times more than COCO.+

A Swin backbone gives multi-scale image features; BERT-base gives word features. Six enhancer layers then update both. Each layer runs deformable self-attention on the image tokens, plain self-attention on the text tokens (with the sub-sentence mask from block 04), image-to-text and text-to-image cross-attention, and a feed-forward network on each side.

X_img, X_txt = Swin(I), BERT(T) for each of 6 layers: X_img = DeformSelfAttn(X_img) X_txt = SelfAttn(X_txt, mask = sub_sentence_mask) X_img, X_txt = BiCrossAttn(X_img, X_txt) X_img, X_txt = FFN(X_img), FFN(X_txt)

Notation ours, following Section 3.1 and fuse_modules.py.

A detail from the code

The bi-attention clamps attention logits to plus or minus 50000 so it can run in half precision (fuse_modules.py, the clamp_min_for_underflow and clamp_max_for_overflow flags). Worth knowing if you change the fusion and start seeing NaNs.

03Language-guided query selection, and the 900-query budgetQueries are the image tokens that best match any word; everything else is dropped.+

After the encoder, every image token is scored against every word token. The token's score is its maximum over words, and the top Nq = 900 tokens are kept. Their positions give the initial anchor boxes; the content part of each query is a learned embedding, as in DINO's mixed query selection.

s_i = max_j ( x_img_i . x_txt_j ) over all word tokens j Q = topk_i ( s_i , k = 900 )

Section 3.2; implemented in transformer.py as enc_outputs_class_unselected.max(-1) followed by torch.topk.

Why this is the line that matters for small objects

The budget is per image, not per object or per area. On a 4000 pixel aerial frame with hundreds of vehicles, all of them compete for 900 slots with each other and with background that happens to look like the text. The paper never varies Nq or reports recall by object size, so the effect is unmeasured.

04Cross-modality decoder and sub-sentence text featuresTwo small changes: a text cross-attention in each decoder layer, and a mask that keeps category names apart.+

Each of the six decoder layers is a DINO decoder layer with one extra block: after self-attention and deformable image cross-attention, the query attends to the word features, then a feed-forward network (Section 3.3; self.ca_text in transformer.py).

When many category names are concatenated into one prompt, plain self-attention lets "car" attend to "boat", which entangles unrelated classes. The sub-sentence mask makes text attention block-diagonal: tokens attend only inside their own phrase, with phrases split at the special tokens [CLS], [SEP], . and ?, and position ids restart in each phrase (Section 3.4; generate_masks_with_special_tokens_and_transfer_map in bertwarper.py).

Practical consequence

The period is not punctuation, it is the class separator. A prompt written as "car, boat, tank" is one phrase to this model. preprocess_caption lowercases the prompt and appends a final period, but does not split commas.

05Loss and inferenceTraining uses a contrastive word loss with focal weighting; inference is two thresholds.+

Training (Section 3.5): L1 and GIoU losses on boxes, a contrastive classification loss between each query and the word tokens with sigmoid focal loss, Hungarian matching on the same costs, and auxiliary losses after every decoder layer and the encoder outputs. Matching and loss weights are listed in Table 8 below.

Inference, from util/inference.py:

logits = sigmoid( query . word ) shape (900, 256) keep box if max_over_words(logits) > box_threshold phrase = the words whose logit > text_threshold

The README uses box_threshold = 0.35 and text_threshold = 0.25; the demo script defaults to 0.3 and 0.25. For COCO-style AP evaluation the thresholds are not applied, all 900 queries are ranked.

Where the code and the paper diverge

No training code. The repository contains no loss, matcher or training script. The contrastive focal loss of Section 3.5 cannot be checked or reused from this release; sigmoid_focal_loss is imported but the criterion is not there.

Prompts are truncated at 256 tokens without a warning. groundingdino.py slices the tokens and the text features to max_text_len = 256. A long aerial class list loses its last classes silently; LVIS's 1203 names cannot fit in one prompt, and how the paper splits them is not in the release.

Small number mismatch. The README says the COCO zero-shot script should give 48.5 for the T checkpoint, the paper reports 48.4.

Leftover settings. The config carries DINO's denoising-training fields (dn_box_noise_scale, dn_label_noise_ratio and others), which do nothing at inference.

08Weak points, and what a reviewer would push onCategory overlap behind "zero-shot", no size breakdown, and a recipe nobody can rerun.+
  1. Zero-shot is category-overlapped. O365 covers most COCO classes, so the headline 52.5 measures domain transfer more than open-set ability. There is no evaluation on categories held out from all pre-training data.
  2. No breakdown by object size. COCO APS is not reported in the tables read here, and nothing on aerial or remote-sensing data.
  3. Small ablation margins, single runs. Most components move COCO by under 1 AP with no variance reported (Table 7).
  4. Weak on the long tail. Below GLIP-T on LVIS rare with matched data (Table 3).
  5. Not reproducible from the release. No training code, and the large-scale data mixes (Cap4M pseudo-labels) are hard to rebuild.
  6. The B checkpoint is not zero-shot on ODinW. It was trained with ODinW-35 and COCO in its data (README checkpoint table), so using it as a zero-shot baseline on those benchmarks would be wrong.
09What to do with this, for your submissionRun it as your baseline, tiled and untiled, and measure what the query budget costs.+

It is your baseline, so run it two ways

Use the T checkpoint (OGC, no COCO or ODinW in its data) on your aerial set as it comes, and again with image tiling. The gap between the two is a direct measure of how much small-object recall the 900-query budget and the input resolution cost.

Measure recall by object size and by query budget

The paper never varies Nq. A recall-versus-Nq curve on small objects is cheap (one config value at inference) and would be a new, clean result for your related-work section or a motivating figure.

Split class prompts deliberately

Keep each prompt under 256 tokens, separate classes with " . ", and state the chunking in your protocol so the comparison is fair.

Do-not-claim register

Do not claim to be the first to fuse language inside the detector encoder or decoder: this paper does both, and GLIP fused before it. Do not call Grounding DINO's COCO numbers "novel class" results when comparing; call them zero-shot transfer.
